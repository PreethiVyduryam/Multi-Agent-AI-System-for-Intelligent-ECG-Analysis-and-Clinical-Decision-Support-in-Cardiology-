Clinical Findings
- 59-year-old female.
- Intermittent, exertional chest discomfort.
- Shortness of breath.
- Increased fatigue.
- Medical history of hypertension and hyperlipidaemia.
- Vital signs: blood pressure 152/92 mmHg, heart rate 96 bpm, oxygen saturation 96%.
- ECG: non-specific ST-T wave changes reported.

Clinical Interpretation
- The combination of exertional chest discomfort, shortness of breath, and fatigue in a patient with hypertension and hyperlipidaemia may suggest an underlying cardiac condition, but the features are non-diagnostic.
- The blood pressure is elevated and heart rate is at the upper range, which could increase myocardial oxygen demand and make exertional symptoms more noticeable.
- Non-specific ST-T wave changes on the ECG are not diagnostic but could be associated with several cardiac processes, including myocardial ischaemia, hypertensive heart disease, or left ventricular hypertrophy.
- The oxygen saturation of 96% is reassuring but does not rule out a cardiac cause for dyspnoea or fatigue.
- Overall, the findings are consistent with a presentation that warrants further investigation, but they do not confirm a specific diagnosis.

Possible Cardiac Considerations
- Consideration 1: Stable angina due to obstructive coronary artery disease
  Reason: Exertional chest discomfort, together with hypertension and hyperlipidaemia, may suggest myocardial ischaemia. The non-specific ST-T changes could be an ECG marker of underlying ischaemia, though not definitive.
- Consideration 2: Heart failure, particularly with preserved ejection fraction
  Reason: Shortness of breath and fatigue are common presenting symptoms of heart failure. Hypertension is a major risk factor. NICE and ESC guidance highlight that such symptoms require assessment of cardiac structure and function, for example with echocardiography.
- Consideration 3: Hypertensive heart disease with left ventricular hypertrophy
  Reason: Long-standing hypertension can lead to structural cardiac changes, and the elevated blood pressure plus non-specific ST-T changes may be consistent with left ventricular hypertrophy or strain. This could also contribute to exertional symptoms.

Important Differential Considerations
- Pulmonary causes of exertional dyspnoea and chest discomfort, such as obstructive lung disease, pulmonary embolism, or pulmonary hypertension, should be considered because symptoms are not specific to a cardiac origin.
- Anaemia or thyroid dysfunction may produce fatigue, exertional breathlessness, and reduced exercise tolerance and should not be overlooked.
- Musculoskeletal chest wall pain or deconditioning could explain some exertional symptoms, particularly if the chest discomfort has atypical features, but further history is needed.
- Cardiac microvascular dysfunction or coronary vasospasm are additional possibilities, especially in a post-menopausal woman with risk factors, though not confirmed by the available data.

Evidence Relevance
- The NICE (2018) and ESC (2023) heart failure guidelines are relevant because the patient’s symptoms of breathlessness and fatigue overlap with heart failure presentations. These guidelines support the need for clinical evaluation, laboratory testing, and echocardiography to differentiate cardiac causes from other conditions.
- The NICE hypertension guideline is relevant because the patient has elevated blood pressure, which contributes to overall cardiovascular risk and may influence the interpretation of symptoms.
- The retrieved evidence does not provide specific information about ECG interpretation, coronary artery disease, or non-specific ST-T changes; therefore, it is insufficient to confirm or exclude any particular cardiac diagnosis.

Information Gaps
- Character of the chest discomfort such as quality, location, radiation, duration, and relieving factors, including whether it consistently occurs with exertion and resolves with rest.
- Need to know if symptoms are stable or progressive, and if any episodes occur at rest.
- Prior cardiac investigations, including previous ECGs, stress tests, or echocardiography.
- Current medications, particularly antihypertensives, statins, or antiplatelet agents.
- Laboratory results: full blood count, renal function, thyroid function, lipid profile, and natriuretic peptides such as NT-proBNP or BNP.
- Echocardiography findings, including left ventricular ejection fraction, wall motion abnormalities, diastolic function, and valve disease.
- Physical examination details, including heart sounds, lung fields, peripheral oedema, and vascular examination.
- Family history of premature cardiovascular disease and menopausal status.

Clinical Reasoning Summary
- This middle-aged woman presents with exertional chest discomfort, dyspnoea, and fatigue against a background of hypertension and hyperlipidaemia, with elevated blood pressure and non-specific ST-T wave changes. These features may suggest myocardial ischaemia or structural heart disease, but they are not diagnostic. Important alternative causes, particularly pulmonary, metabolic, and musculoskeletal conditions, should also be considered.
- Further clinical assessment, including detailed history, examination, laboratory tests, and cardiac imaging, is required to clarify the cause. Because exertional cardiac symptoms in a patient with multiple cardiovascular risk factors can indicate significant disease, clinician review is recommended in a timely and appropriate setting.
