Clinical Findings
- 45-year-old male.
- Symptoms: intermittent episodes of a racing heartbeat, associated dizziness, and fatigue.
- Medical history: hypertension.
- Vital signs: heart rate 108 bpm, blood pressure 140/88 mmHg, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.
- ECG: “Occasional premature beats reported.”

Clinical Interpretation
- The heart rate of 108 bpm is above the normal resting range and may be consistent with the patient’s report of a racing heartbeat.
- Palpitations with dizziness may suggest a possible arrhythmia, including rapid heart rhythms that could temporarily reduce cardiac output.
- Intermittent symptoms are nonspecific but may indicate a paroxysmal tachyarrhythmia or clinically significant ectopy.
- ECG showing occasional premature beats is a finding, but it does not confirm a specific rhythm disorder.
- A history of hypertension is relevant because it increases baseline cardiovascular risk and may influence the likelihood of structural or electrical heart disease.
- Oxygen saturation is normal, but this does not exclude arrhythmia as a cause of symptoms.

Possible Cardiac Considerations
- Consideration 1: Paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia, including atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter
  Reason: Intermittent episodes of a racing heartbeat, dizziness, and an elevated heart rate may be consistent with a paroxysmal tachyarrhythmia. Hypertension is a relevant risk factor for atrial fibrillation.
- Consideration 2: Ventricular ectopy or non-sustained ventricular arrhythmia
  Reason: The ECG reports occasional premature beats, and the patient reports dizziness. Premature ventricular beats can occasionally be associated with more significant ventricular arrhythmias.
- Consideration 3: Symptomatic sinus tachycardia
  Reason: The heart rate of 108 bpm may simply represent sinus tachycardia, which could be triggered by anemia, thyroid dysfunction, stress, infection, or medication effects. The symptoms may be related to the rapid rate rather than a primary arrhythmia.

Important Differential Considerations
- Anemia: May cause fatigue, palpitations, and dizziness with sinus tachycardia.
- Thyroid disease, especially hyperthyroidism: Can cause palpitations, tachycardia, and fatigue.
- Anxiety or panic episodes: May produce intermittent palpitations, dizziness, and a racing heartbeat.
- Electrolyte abnormalities: Could predispose to ectopy and palpitations, especially potassium or magnesium disturbances.
- Medication-related causes: Antihypertensive medications or other agents can cause heart rate changes or dizziness.

Evidence Relevance
- The NICE guidance on atrial fibrillation is relevant because it highlights the need for 12-lead ECG to confirm suspected arrhythmia and supports ambulatory rhythm monitoring when symptoms are intermittent.
- The NICE and ESC hypertension guidance is relevant to the patient’s history of hypertension and supports accurate blood pressure measurement and cardiovascular risk assessment.
- The retrieved evidence does not directly address premature beats or the specific evaluation of palpitations with intermittent symptoms, so evidence is insufficient to confirm a diagnosis.

Information Gaps
- Full 12-lead ECG interpretation, including rhythm, rate, QRS duration, QTc, and whether premature beats are atrial or ventricular.
- Duration and frequency of palpitation episodes, and whether dizziness occurs with palpitations or only during racing episodes.
- Any history of syncope or near-syncope.
- Whether angina, chest pain, or shortness of breath accompanies symptoms.
- Current medications, including antihypertensive therapy.
- Home blood pressure readings and degree of blood pressure control.
- Previous cardiac investigations or known structural heart disease.
- Family history of arrhythmia, sudden cardiac death, or cardiomyopathy.
- Laboratory results, including electrolytes, thyroid function, hemoglobin, and renal function.
- Triggers such as caffeine, alcohol, stimulant use, sleep deprivation, or stress.

Clinical Reasoning Summary
- This patient presents with intermittent palpitations, dizziness, fatigue, elevated heart rate, and a history of hypertension. The ECG finding of occasional premature beats is nonspecific but does not rule out a significant arrhythmia.
- The symptom pattern may suggest a possible paroxysmal tachyarrhythmia or an arrhythmia-related cause of dizziness, but no confirmed diagnosis can be made from the available information.
- Further clinical assessment, including a 12-lead ECG and likely ambulatory rhythm monitoring, is appropriate to clarify the cause of intermittent symptoms.
- Urgent clinician review is advised, particularly if episodes are prolonged, associated with syncope, chest pain, or severe symptoms.
